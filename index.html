01 · Main project

Which U.S. hospitals carry the most unpaid care?

After a small accident, my hospital bill was far higher than I expected. Insurance covered most of it, and I still owed part. I wondered what happens when a patient can't pay at all. The hospital absorbs the cost, and that is called uncompensated care.

Guess first

Which hospitals carry more of it?

Result

Urban

Urban hospitals carried about 3x more unpaid care in dollars. Hospital size, measured in beds, explains most of that.

Data

22,745

rows after cleaning

Period

2019–23

CMS hospital cost reports (HCRIS)

Model fit

R² 0.998

Random Forest, vs 0.49 for linear regression

What drives the result

#1 Unpaid care per bed (a ratio I created)
Hospital size (beds), which the ratio is built on
Urban or rural, payer mix, state, year

The short bar marks other inputs I have not ranked here. The per-bed ratio did not exist in the raw data. I built it from 27 raw columns, which became 30 features.

So what

Funders should look at hospital size, not location alone. Administrators can compare themselves with hospitals of similar size.

What the data can't say

Per bed, the picture flips: rural hospitals carry about $43K per bed against $40K for urban. So rural hospitals carry a heavier load for their size, even though urban ones carry more in total. The 2020 to 2021 dip reflects lower patient volume and federal relief funding, not a real improvement.

03 · Marketing analytics

Predict how customers react, then check it against reality

A business is about to raise a price or change a policy. Real surveys are slow and expensive. This app runs a simulated survey in under a minute, then searches the web for what real customers did and compares the two.

Try it · same 20 simulated customers, two Netflix price rises

Accept50%
Reduce (downgrade)50%
Reject (cancel)0%
Delay0%

The crowd reacts to the size of the increase. Development run, so treat exact percentages as rough.

How it works

5

choices per persona: accept, reduce, delay, switch, reject. Each one explains why in its own words.

Real test · Netflix password sharing, 2023

Netflix gained subscribers even though many customers said they were unhappy. My first version predicted customers would leave, the wrong direction.

After I added a "customer status" to each persona (paying, non-paying, or not a customer), the prediction became a partial match. In a 100-persona run the simulator saw no clear change, which still underestimates how many password borrowers started paying.

What I got wrong, and fixed

At first nobody ever chose "switch", even for an iPhone price rise. The options were always listed in the same order, so the model favored the first one. I shuffled the order for each persona and clarified the choices.

Switching then showed up where it should: 2.5% for iPhone, 24% for a small coffee brand.

Built to be checked

The personas never see the real-world evidence, so they can't copy the real outcome. If the evidence doesn't clearly describe the exact event, the app shows no verdict at all. Runs used 20 to 100 personas, so the percentages are rough.
